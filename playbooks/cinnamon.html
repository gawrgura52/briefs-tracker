Who we sell to

The person who got hurt

  • Story ads (12 of 27, 62% of spend) star the person hurt by someone close: spouse, sister, friends, a child.
  • Women 50 to 61 carry most story spend; men from their 30s to 60 work too (protector, humiliated at his own event).
  • What they want back is themselves: 11 of 12 stories end on the old self returning, in front of the people who hurt them.
The hook

Open mid-wound, no product

  • A line said by or to the hurt person, at the worst moment, no setup. Best: "I came home 40 minutes early." (64% hook rate).
  • Blood sugar is never the hook in a story ad.
  • 3 hooks per script; several winners are a specific hook version.
The reveal

A peer who has been there

  • Hands over the pouch at about 30 to 40% (8 of 12 stories, 83% of story spend). Usually older, looks younger.
  • Doctors are the obstacle in 7 stories, never the hero. White-coat experts all stayed under $15K.
The order

Same beats every time

  • Public deadline, failed fixes, the peer, mechanism (~40%), first ingredient (~50%), week by week, public payoff (~73%), brand name (~78%), lead to camera with the pouch.
  • Results: felt in week 1 (sleep, no crash), seen by week 3 to 5, then other people notice.
The mechanism

Nearly fixed lines

  • Ceylon vs cassia, "a completely different plant": 23 of 27 ads, 87% of spend.
  • The doors (60% of spend) and the switch (57%). Told 3 times in a story. "Two capsules every morning" in 17 ads.
The close

One offer

  • 30-day guarantee (85% of spend), "up to 70% off" (64%), doses spoken in 19 ads.
  • Hook and hold rates don't predict spend here. The top 5 just avoid weight numbers, sexual or violent scenes and expert heads.

Resilia rules to strip when porting

Many winners were made before these rules. When you port the structure, leave these parts behind. Full detail in cin.md.

Nov 4: US origin
  • No US flags anywhere, no FDA marks, no "made in the USA", "ships from the US", "FDA-registered", "lab-tested in the USA", "GMP-certified" (on hold).
  • OK: "Third-party tested for purity and potency".
  • Breaks it: #3, #9, #11, #13, #15, #21.
Sep 28: CIN compliance
  • No disease names on a character (diabetes, prediabetic, neuropathy...). Insulin resistance and PCOS can be named.
  • No A1C numbers, no product lowering a number, no medication named, stopped or beaten.
  • Up to 3 symptoms per video. "Clinically studied", never "proven".
Oct: brand line
  • No minors in adult context, no sexual content as the hook, no religion or ethnicity as the reason it works, no real people, no family sexual themes, no shaming a protected trait, no competitor defamation, no gore.
  • No one implied to be the founder (breaks it: #9, #23).
  • Screenshot test: would Johnathan want his name under it? Zayd decides.
Oct 4: story framework
  • No parasites, no made-up biology, no weight numbers (breaks it: #17, #24, #25).
  • Product lines: "[ingredient] supports [function], and [felt result]". The body does the work.
